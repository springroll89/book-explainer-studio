Chapter One: The Last Crossing

Mara reached the ferry after the final bell. The boat was still tied to the post, but its keeper had gone home. Across the river, a single lantern shone in the window of the house where her brother waited.

She could not swim. The river had risen over the stones that usually made a shallow crossing. She found an empty bucket beside the landing and called toward the opposite bank, but the wind carried her voice downstream.

Then the lantern moved. It crossed the window once, disappeared, and crossed it again. Mara remembered their childhood signal: two passes meant stay where you are. She sat beneath the roof of the landing and watched the water climb the lowest step.

Chapter Two: At First Light

The keeper returned at dawn. He saw Mara waiting and pointed to a rope stretched high between two trees. During the night, her brother had used it to send a small basket across the river. The basket hung above her, hidden by the landing roof, with a note inside.

The note said that the bridge upstream had washed away and that he was safe. He had moved the lantern twice because he could not make his words reach her. Mara had seen the warning, but not the basket.

She took the note with her on the morning ferry. On the far bank, her brother was carrying dry blankets toward the landing. They met before either of them had to call across the water again.
